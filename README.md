# Dataset-and-code-for-recruitment-bottleneck-and-marsh-collapse

## Overview

This repository contains data and R code supporting the article **"Recruitment bottlenecks and reinforcing environmental degradation drive marsh collapse"** by Man Qi and Keryn Gedan, accepted in *Ecology*.

The study combines: (1) a vegetation quadrat survey across four deteriorating marsh platforms at Deal Island, Chesapeake Bay, USA; and (2) a 2019-2021 transplant experiment with the flood-tolerant rush *Juncus roemerianus* at Deal Island and Farm Creek Marsh. The study tests whether species replacement is constrained by competition, dispersal/colonization limitation, and physical degradation associated with peat collapse and prolonged inundation.

The vegetation survey was conducted in August 2018 in Deal Island. At each of four marsh platforms in Deal Island, three 150-m transects extended from the vegetated marsh toward ponded areas, with five 1 x 1 m quadrats (sampling points A-E) per transect. Percent cover of plant species, bare ground, open water, and water depth were recorded. These survey data were previously described in Qi et al. (2021) and are re-analyzed here at the functional-group level.

The transplant experiment began in April 2019 at Deal Island and Farm Creek Marsh. *J. roemerianus* transplants consisted of 20 x 20 x 20 cm blocks of culms and surrounding soil. Treatments represented different stages of marsh deterioration and experimental manipulations of competition and elevation. Plant performance was monitored in April, June. July, August, September in 2019, June and September in 2020, and June in 2021.

### Study sites of the J. roemerianus transplant experiment

- **Deal Island**: deteriorating brackish marsh on the Manokin River, Maryland, USA; generally more saline than Farm Creek Marsh.
- **Farm Creek Marsh**: deteriorating brackish marsh on Fishing Bay, Maryland, USA; generally less saline than Deal Island.

### Main treatment labels of the J. roemerianus transplant experiment

- **Pond control**: transplant placed in a pond at ambient pond elevation.
- **Pond elevated**: transplant placed in a pond with substrate elevation experimentally raised toward pre-collapse marsh elevation.
- **Dieback**: transplant placed in a vegetation dieback patch where obvious peat collapse had not yet occurred.
- **Neighbor intact**: transplant placed in vegetated marsh with neighboring vegetation left intact.
- **Neighbor removal**: transplant placed in vegetated marsh with neighboring vegetation within 0.5 m clipped monthly.
- **Juncus patch**: transplant returned to its original/source *J. roemerianus* patch.

### Flood-tolerance groups used in the vegetation analysis

The R code classifies the following species as **Flood-Sen** (flood-sensitive): *Phragmites australis*, *Spartina patens*, *Distichlis spicata*, and *Iva frutescens*. Other retained species are classified as **Flood-Tol** (flood-tolerant), including *Bolboschoenus fluviatilis*, *Schoenoplectus americanus*, *Spartina alterniflora*, and *Juncus roemerianus*. Flood-tolerance rankings follow Qi et al. (2021a).

## Repository structure

- `Code/` - R scripts used for data processing, statistical analyses, and figures.
- `Data/Vegetation survey/` - 2018 quadrat vegetation and environmental survey data.
- `Data/Pond plant transplanting result-2019/` - transplant performance, salinity, redox, and soil-strength data for Deal Island and Farm Creek Marsh.
- `Data/Groundwater/` - water-level logger records, well metadata, and tide-gauge data used to calculate hydroperiod.
- `Data/RTK elevation survey 2019/` - RTK-GPS elevation measurements at transplant locations and groundwater wells.
- `Result/` - selected figure outputs generated by the R scripts.
- `Dataset and code for recruitment bottleneck and marsh collapse.Rproj` - RStudio project file.

## Data files and metadata

### 1. `Data/Vegetation survey/Plant diversity source data.xlsx`

**Purpose:** Source data for the 2018 vegetation survey and Fig. 2. Used to calculate dieback area, species abundance, species occupancy, and functional-group occupancy along the marsh deterioration gradient.

**Sampling unit:** One 1 x 1 m quadrat. There are four marsh platforms (`Site` S1-S4), three transects per platform (`Transect` T1-T3), and five positions along each transect (`Point` A-E).

**Sheet `Percent cover(%)`:**

| Column | Description | Units / coding |
|---|---|---|
| first unnamed column | Original field/site-transect identifier (e.g., DIS1T1 means Deal Island, Site 1, Transect 1) or survey date (in format of dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm) where recorded | identifier/date |
| `Site` | Marsh platform identifier | S1, S2, S3, S4 |
| `Transect` | Replicate transect within marsh platform | T1, T2, T3 |
| `Point` | Position along the vegetated-to-pond gradient | A-E; A is toward vegetated/upland marsh and E is closest to pond |
| species columns | Estimated cover of each listed plant species in the quadrat | percent cover (%) |
| `Open Water` | Open-water cover within quadrat | % |
| `Bare ground` | Unvegetated exposed substrate within quadrat | % |

Species columns are: `Phragmites australis`, `Spartina patens`, `Distichlis spicata`, `Iva frutescens`, `Bolboschoenus fluviatilis`, `Schoenoplectus americanus`, `Spartina alterniflora`, `Juncus roemerianus`, `Salicornia sp.`, `Fimbristylis spadicea`, `Setaria geniculate`, and `Pluchea odorata`.

**Derived variables in code:**

- `Dieback_area = Open Water + Bare ground`.
- Species-level occupancy at each Site x Point is the percentage of the three replicate transects/quadrats where abundance is >0.
- Functional-group occupancy is the percentage of the three replicate transects/quadrats where at least one member of the flood-tolerance group is present.
- Species occurring in fewer than two quadrats or never reaching 10% cover are excluded by `Code/occupancy vs abundance.R` from the plotted occupancy/abundance analysis.

**Manuscript links:** Fig. 2a (dieback area), Fig. 2b (functional-group occupancy), Fig. 2c (species abundance and occupancy); Results section "Plant community composition shifts along hydrological stress gradients".

### 5. `Data/RTK elevation survey 2019/RTK data of DI and FCM.xls`

**Purpose:** RTK-GPS elevation measurements used to compare elevation among sites, marsh zones, and pond-elevation treatments (Fig. 3a), and to reference groundwater levels to NAVD88.

**Vertial Datum:** NAVD 1988, using Geoid 12B, as reported in the manuscript.
**Coordinate system:**The coordinate system used in the RTK is US State Plane 1983, 2011 Adjustment.  The Zone is Maryland 1900. 

The workbook contains three sheets.

#### Sheet `Transplanting waypoints`

| Column | Description | Units / coding |
|---|---|---|
| `Transplant ID` | Individual RTK measurement identifier | identifier |
| `X cord` | Horizontal coordinate recorded by RTK system | coordinate; coordinate reference system should be confirmed by authors |
| `Y cord` | Horizontal coordinate recorded by RTK system | coordinate; coordinate reference system should be confirmed by authors |
| `Elevation (m)` | Ground/substrate elevation | m, NAVD88 |
| `Note` | Transplant waypoint grouping; multiple RTK readings share the same transplant point | identifier |
| `Site` | Marsh site | Deal Island or Farm Creek Marsh |
| `Treatment` | Marsh zone or experimental treatment associated with waypoint | e.g., Dieback, Pond control, Pond elevated, Neighbor patch, Juncus patch |

The analysis averages replicate RTK measurements by `Note`, `Site`, and `Treatment`, then fits an LMM with Site, Treatment, and their interaction as fixed effects and transplant point (`Note`) as a random intercept.

#### Sheet `Groundwater well`

Same column structure as above, with `Treatment` identifying well base/top measurements for pond, dieback, and neighboring/healthy marsh wells. These measurements support conversion of logger depths to water elevation relative to NAVD88.

#### Sheet `FCM_pond treatment`

Same column structure as `Transplanting waypoints`; contains additional Farm Creek Marsh pond/treatment elevation measurements.

**Manuscript links:** Fig. 3a; elevation analysis in Appendix S1 Table S2.

## R code

All scripts use relative paths under the RStudio project. For reproducibility, open `Dataset and code for recruitment bottleneck and marsh collapse.Rproj` and run scripts with the repository root as the working directory.

### `Code/occupancy vs abundance.R`

**Inputs:**
- `Data/Vegetation survey/Plant diversity source data.xlsx`
- `Data/Vegetation survey/Pore water Salinity, pH, Redox, water depth.xlsx` (`clean data`)

**Purpose:**
- calculates dieback area and 90% confidence intervals for Fig. 2a;
- reshapes species-cover data;
- filters rare/low-abundance species;
- calculates species-level abundance and occupancy for Fig. 2c;
- calculates functional-group occupancy for Fig. 2b;
- fits the occupancy LMM: `Occupancy ~ FloodClass * Point + (1 | Site)`;
- performs DHARMa residual diagnostics;
- creates Fig. 2 components.

**Outputs in `Result/`:**
- `Occupancy difference between flood tolerance groups.tiff`
- `Occupancy_abundance by site.tiff` (combined Fig. 2)

**Manuscript links:** Fig. 2; H1; Appendix S1 Table S2 occupancy statistics.

### `Code/community level_water depth and dieback ara.R`

**Inputs:** same two vegetation-survey workbooks above.

**Purpose:** statistical tests for dieback area and water depth across Site, Point, and their interaction. The script checks assumptions, considers log transformations, and uses aligned-rank-transform analysis for water depth when required.

**Manuscript links:** Fig. 2a; Appendix S1 Table S2 (water depth and dieback area).

### `Code/Relative growth rate of height and shoot number.R`

**Inputs:**
- `Data/Pond plant transplanting result-2019/DI.xlsx`
- `Data/Pond plant transplanting result-2019/FCM-Control.xlsx`

**Purpose:** processes transplant shoot number, shoot height, flowering, biomass, and survival data; calculates relative changes in plant performance; conducts treatment/site analyses and post-hoc comparisons; and produces plant-performance figures.

The accepted manuscript uses relative shoot-number increase, final inflorescence number, and survival as the primary plant-performance responses. The script contains additional exploratory/legacy analyses of height and biomass that are not central to the accepted manuscript.

**Outputs in `Result/`:**
- `Shoot height and number.tiff`
- `survival rate_series.tiff`
- `plant performance in 2021.tiff`
- related plant-performance outputs retained in the repository

**Manuscript links:** Fig. 5; H2-H4; Appendix S1 Fig. S3 and Table S2.

### `Code/hydroperiod and soil variables.R`

**Inputs:** groundwater logger files, `Well info.txt`, `bishop.csv`, transplant-workbook `Salinity` and `Redox and Soil strength` sheets, and RTK elevation workbook.

**Purpose:**
- converts logger depth to water elevation relative to NAVD88 and soil surface;
- calculates hydroperiod as the fraction of observations with water above the soil surface;
- analyzes soil redox potential and soil strength;
- analyzes porewater salinity with an LMM (`Salinity_.ppt. ~ Site + (1 | Month)`);
- analyzes elevation with LMMs including Site, Treatment, and their interaction and transplant point as a random intercept;
- produces hydrology/environmental-variable plots.

**Manuscript links:** Figs. 3-4; environmental differences between sites; Appendix S1 Table S2.

**Reproducibility note:** this script currently contains `setwd(path_dir(here::here()))`. If the script is run from the supplied `.Rproj`, users may need to replace this with `setwd(here::here())` (or remove the `setwd` call) so that the relative `Data/...` paths resolve from the repository root.


## Derived variables and statistical models used in the accepted article

### Functional-group occupancy

For each Site x Point x FloodClass combination, presence is first determined within each of the three replicate transects. Occupancy is then:

`100 x (number of replicate quadrats/transects with >=1 species in the functional group present) / 3`.

The accepted analysis uses an LMM fitted with `lme4`, with flood-tolerance group and sampling point as fixed effects and marsh platform as a random intercept. DHARMa is used for simulation-based residual diagnostics.

### Relative shoot-number increase

The plant-performance script calculates proportional change in shoot number between monitoring periods as `(later shoot number - initial shoot number) / initial shoot number`. The accepted manuscript describes the relative change across the experimental period as the indicator of clonal reproduction. Users should follow the final manuscript definition when reproducing Fig. 5 because the script also retains exploratory calculations for intermediate monitoring dates.

### Hydroperiod

Hydroperiod is calculated as the fraction of valid water-level observations for which water level relative to the soil surface is >0.

## Software and R packages

Analyses were conducted in R. The repository scripts load the following packages (not every package is required by every script):

- `lme4`, `lmerTest`, `DHARMa` - linear mixed-effects models, fixed-effect tests, and diagnostics
- `ARTool` - aligned rank transform analyses
- `car` - Levene tests and supporting analyses
- `emmeans`, `multcomp`, `multcompView`, `DescTools`, `agricolae`, `FSA`, `rcompanion`, `rstatix` - post-hoc tests and statistical utilities
- `readxl` - Excel import
- `dplyr`, `tidyr`, `tidyverse` - data manipulation
- `ggplot2`, `ggtext`, `patchwork`, `Rmisc` - summaries and figures
- `lubridate`, `hydrostats` - date/time and hydrological processing
- `here`, `fs` - project-relative paths
- `vegan` - loaded by the vegetation-analysis script

For exact reproducibility, users should record their installed R and package versions when re-running the analysis. The manuscript cites R (2024), `lme4` (Bates et al. 2015), and `DHARMa` (Hartig 2024).

## Missing values and data-entry conventions

- The transplant scripts read `NA`, `missing`, and blank cells as missing values where specified.
- `NA` in `Well info.txt` denotes unavailable/not applicable measurements.
- Blank spreadsheet cells generally indicate measurements not taken or not applicable; consult the treatment and sampling design before interpreting structural blanks as missing observations.
- Field-note columns contain qualitative observations and are not generally used as quantitative response variables.
